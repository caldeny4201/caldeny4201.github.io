The Fixed-Count Structure One Page. Every Count. Every Rule. Every Exclusion. Print this. Pin it above your desk. Fill it in before you build anything. --- The Prerequisites Before you fill in a single layer, answer these three questions: 1. What is the generic activity? The boring thing that already exists. Bookkeeping. Cleaning. Maintenance. HR admin. Payroll. Design. SEO. 2. Who is the specific buyer? Not "small businesses." Not "professionals." A named type of buyer with a named problem. 3. What is the recurring monthly pain? The thing that comes back every month that the buyer would pay to make disappear. If you cannot answer all three, stop. Go have ten conversations first. --- The Fixed Counts Element Count Target audience 1 Entry offers 2 (one free, one paid) Monthly tiers 3 (Essential, Insights, Partner) Unique high-value module 1 Scope definitions 4 Proof of Value 1 (quarterly deliverable) Guarantees 10–13 Process steps 7 Policy modals 7 Referral reward 1 (after 2nd invoice) Deals / discounts 0 Memberships / loyalty ladders 0 Free professional work 0 Every count is deliberate. Every count is load-bearing. --- Layer 1 — Brand Fill in: · Brand name: ______________ · Brand mark: Single letter in serif on dark background · Brand subtitle: ______________ (5 words or fewer, format: [Activity] for [audience]) · Tagline: ______________ (one sentence, optional) The subtitle formula: Monthly books for physios. Maintenance coordination for retail property managers. HR admin for restaurants. Done test: A stranger reads the name and subtitle and can say what the business does and who it serves within five seconds. --- Layer 2 — Hero Fill in: · Headline: ______________ (one promise) · Sub-headline: ______________ (2–3 sentences) · Primary CTA: ______________ (one action) · Trust strip: 4 signals · Hero panel: 4 numbered steps (01–04) The headline formula: [Outcome the buyer wants] [in the timeframe they want it]. The steps formula: 01 — What the client sends. 02 — What you do first. 03 — What you produce. 04 — What happens next. Done test: A prospect reads the hero and can describe what they get, in what timeframe, and what the next step is. In one sentence. --- Layer 3 — Focused Audience Fill in: · Primary niche: ______________ (one sentence) · Specific targets in order (5): ______________ · The specific problems you solve (5–7): ______________ · "Not for us" list (4–6): ______________ The niche formula: [Buyer type] with [specific characteristic] in [geographic scope] who [specific pain]. Done test: A prospect in the niche reads the panel and thinks "this was written for me." A prospect outside the niche thinks "this is not for me." Both reactions are correct. --- Layer 4 — Pricing Fill in: Tier Price Scope Per-unit Essential R______ ______ R______ Insights (Recommended) R______ ______ R______ Partner R______ ______ R______ · What's included in every tier: ______________ · What's tier-specific: ______________ · What's not included in any tier (5–8): ______________ · Scope definitions (4): one transaction, one account, one entity, work beyond scope. The tier formula: · Essential = entry plan for small volume · Insights = natural landing point · Partner = management visibility The per-unit rule: Every tier should have roughly the same per-unit cost. If Insights is cheaper per unit than Essential, you've mispriced. Done test: A prospect knows within 30 seconds which tier fits them, what it costs, and what they get. --- Layer 5 — Unique High-Value Module Fill in: · The unique module: ______________ · What it does (2–3 sentences): ______________ · What it does not do (1–2 sentences): ______________ · Why it matters: ______________ · Which tiers it's included in: ______________ Examples: Trust account reconciliation. Medical aid remittance reconciliation. Emergency coordination. Compliance tracking. Done test: A prospect in the niche reads the module and thinks "this is the thing I need and nobody else does it for me." --- Layer 6 — Entry Offers Fill in: Free conversation: · Name: ______________ · Duration: ______ minutes · What happens: ______________ · What it is not: ______________ Paid qualifier: · Name: ______________ · Price: R______ · What it produces: ______________ · Credit rule: ______________ The boundary rule: A free conversation is marketing spend. Free professional work is labour spend. Never publish free professional work. Done test: A prospect knows exactly how to start, with zero risk of losing money. --- Layer 7 — Proof of Value Fill in: · What it shows (5–6 items): ______________ · Frequency: Quarterly (monthly for Partner tier) · What it is not: Not a testimonial. Not marketing. Not a promise. Done test: A client can forward the Proof of Value to their partner or board without editing it. --- Layer 8 — Guarantees Fill in 10–13 guarantees: Guarantee Commitment Clear scope ______________ [Unique module] work ______________ Fixed monthly fee No hourly billing. No surprise charges. Delivery commitment ______________ Correction policy ______________ Communication Acknowledged within ______ Named contact ______________ Cancellation One month's written notice. Service recovery ______________ Data protection POPIA-compliant. [Add more to reach 10–13] ______________ What we do not guarantee: ______________ (3–5 items). Done test: Every guarantee is specific, bounded, controlled. Nothing about outcomes you cannot deliver. --- Layer 9 — Compliance Infrastructure Fill in: 7 policy modals: 1. Terms of Engagement 2. Privacy Policy 3. POPIA Notice 4. Unique trust document (Trust Account Statement / Medical Data Statement / Emergency Protocol) 5. Cookie Notice 6. Service Disclaimer 7. Complaints Procedure · Information Officer: ______________ · Data retention period: ______ years · Information Regulator: inforegulator.org.za / complaints.IR@justice.gov.za Done test: Every modal is complete, specific to the business, and reflects the actual service. --- The Rules 1. One audience. More dilutes the pitch. 2. Two entry offers. One free conversation, one paid qualifier. Nothing else public. 3. Three tiers. Two is a binary. Four freezes the buyer. 4. One unique module. Zero makes you generic. Two makes you unfocused. 5. Four scope definitions. Transaction, account, entity, work beyond scope. Prevents 90% of disputes. 6. One Proof of Value. Quarterly. Forwardable. Not a testimonial. 7. Ten to thirteen guarantees. Below ten feels thin. Above thirteen feels like overpromising. 8. Seven process steps. Receive, source, quote, approve, coordinate, follow up, record. Minimum for a complete workflow. 9. Seven policy modals. Full legal infrastructure. Fewer leaves you exposed. 10. One referral reward. R500, paid after the second invoice. Not the first. 11. Zero deals. Deals delay decisions, train buyers to negotiate, attract price-shoppers. 12. Zero memberships. One relationship, one price. No ladders, no loyalty credits. 13. Zero free professional work. Free conversation only. Never free labour. --- The Exclusions Because the counts matter, the exclusions matter too: · No "starting from" prices · No "call for a quote" · No free trial as a public offer · No first-month discount · No annual prepay incentive · No tier-upgrade discount · No loyalty credit · No referral ladder · No membership tiers · No bundles · No free professional work · No urgency devices ("sign within X days") · No scarcity claims ("only 3 spots left") · No negotiation Every one of these is a technique that works in consumer markets and fails in a small service business. --- The Website Structure 18 sections, in this order: 1. Sticky header — brand, nav, two CTAs 2. Hero — headline, sub-headline, trust strip, numbered steps 3. Focused audience — main panel + "not for us" block 4. Pricing — three tier cards 5. Unique module — dark panel inside pricing 6. Scope definitions — four numbered cards 7. Entry offers — two cards 8. Proof of Value — dark panel, two columns 9. Guarantees — table + "what we do not guarantee" 10. Why section — six numbered differentiators 11. Unique module repeat — second dark panel 12. Promotions — four cards 13. Financials — illustrative economics table 14. Process — seven-step grid 15. Compliance strip — green panel, three badges 16. Launch plan — four week cards 17. Final CTA — full-width dark section 18. Footer — four columns Plus 7 policy modals linked from the footer. --- The Sales Process Four mechanisms. No others. Step Count What it is Free conversation 1 15-minute health check or workflow review Paid qualifier 1 R595 diagnostic, credited on signup Closing mechanism 1 First-month pilot — used in sales, not published Referral reward 1 R500 after second invoice Nothing else exists. No discounts, no urgency, no loyalty credits. --- The Order of Operations Do not build the site first. Do not build the policy modals first. Do not register the company first. 1. Talk to ten prospects. Listen. Record their words. Confirm the pain is real. 2. Test the price. Ask what they currently pay. Listen for the number. 3. Find one delivery partner. Confirm rates. 4. Build the one-page offer sheet. Offer, mechanism, boundary, price. 5. Run one pilot. Measure cost. Measure margin. 6. If the pilot works, build the site. 7. If three pilots work, build the infrastructure. Nothing here should be built before the market has spoken. --- The One-Page Offer Summary At the end of all nine layers, the entire offer should fit on one page: [Brand name] [Subtitle] For: [specific audience] The problem: [specific pain] The offer: [outcome in one sentence] The mechanism: [4–7 bullets] The boundary: [3–5 bullets] Pricing: Essential — R______ /month Insights — R______ /month (Recommended) Partner — R______ /month To start: [free conversation]. No obligation. Guarantees: [list 3–5 most important] Cancellation: One month's notice. Records returned. No penalties. If it fits on one page, it is clear. If it does not, keep cutting. --- The Final Test Before you consider this a real business, answer these five questions: 1. Have you spoken to ten prospects in the niche? 2. Have you tested the price with at least five of them? 3. Do you have one delivery partner confirmed at a known rate? 4. Have you run one pilot and measured the actual cost? 5. Do you have three months of runway to survive to the first three clients? Yes to all five = a business. No to any = a plan. A plan is not a business. It is a hypothesis waiting to be tested. --- What This Is Called The Fixed-Count Structure. A service business operating system. Nine layers. Thirteen fixed counts. Thirteen rules. Fourteen exclusions. Eighteen website sections. Seven policy modals. Four sales mechanisms. Every count is deliberate. Every count is load-bearing. Every count was chosen because the alternative — more or fewer — created a specific problem. Most business frameworks tell you how to think. This one tells you what to build, in what order, with what rules. Fill it in. Test it. Then build it.
